# Ditto

Amber's first Pokemon. Fuji's trained weapon during the Pallet chapters. After the timeskip, a copier with a short memory, not a stored arsenal.

## Origin

Ditto aren't a natural species. They're a byproduct of genetic cloning research — Fuji pioneered it, others followed, and every Ditto in the world traces back to that kind of mistake. A cloning attempt goes wrong, produces a blob that can temporarily become the source Pokemon but always imperfectly, and a Ditto exists. They escaped labs, bred, spread. The species is entirely man-made.

The universal defect: every Ditto keeps its own face. Wrong eyes, wrong mouth, Ditto's expression bleeding through whatever form it's wearing. That flaw is baked into all of them — the consistent failure mode of the cloning process itself.

This Ditto is different. It is a failed **Mew** clone.

No face bleed-through. Full morphological fidelity. When it transforms, it gets the strength too, not a weakened copy. Both were thought impossible for a Ditto. Oak says it in ch16: *"Even its strength is copied? I thought Ditto transformations were always incomplete..."*

Mew is the origin of all Pokemon genetics and natively knows Transform. A Mew clone transforms the way Mew does: completely. It can also hold a short memory of recent forms. Standard Ditto cannot.

## What Fuji Made It

Fuji drilled high-value forms until they stayed available on command:

- Gyarados
- Nidoking
- Rhydon
- Gengar
- Arcanine
- Ninetales

Giovanni's heavy hitters and Blaine's ace — Pokemon Fuji had studied or accessed through Team Rocket. "Protocol Five" and similar numbered commands triggered those transformations instantly. That list was current at the Pallet attack because he kept drilling it. [ch13] [ch16]

## What It Actually Is

Under the training: a Mew-descended Pokemon. Mew is curious, playful, life-affirming. That's in there. Fuji built the weapon. He never particularly tried to break what was underneath.

Playfulness is Mew-nature, not a trained trait and not something Amber taught. She gave it room.

## The Killing Arc (ch13–16)

Fuji had used Ditto to fight. He had never ordered it to kill.

Ch13 is the first time:

1. **Dragon Rage on Poliwag** — complies fully
2. **Hyper Beam on Stephen** — visible resistance, then a deliberately weakened shot. Fuji: *"Why did you hold back?"* Stephen is crumpled, not gone.
3. **"Finish them. Bite."** — refuses. Form begins dissolving.
4. Fuji recalls it as useless. Magmar kills without hesitation.

Ditto tried to fire but not kill. Stephen still dies.

By ch15–16, ordered to kill the children and then Oak, it refuses from the start. It can fight. It will fight. It will not cross that floor.

## With Amber

Amber didn't try to bond with Ditto. She was living in Fuji's apartment, scared, figuring things out — and Ditto was there. That was enough.

Ditto chose Amber as its trainer and partner over Fuji. Traveling with her follows from that choice. Playfulness and curiosity are personality, not a travel goal.

At the end of ch16, Oak catches Ditto from the scorched yard. Ditto goes in the ball without resistance. Oak gives it to Amber at the end of ch17.

Amber treats it as a normal game-Ditto until the journey: transform what's in front of you. She does not know about the memory buffer, the Mew origin, or Fuji's protocols as a system. She finds the buffer on the road. Why it can do that stays later.

## After the Pallet years

Drafting. Not yet on the page.

Four years at the ranch mellow it. The guardian edge dulls. It can be careless: late to a threat, too willing to treat a situation as safe. The protector role is still there. Ditto does not always live up to it.

Ranch life already included Amber playing with other Pokemon. Ordinary sharing is not new. Ditto does not want protocol-style drilling; the ranch years did not restore Fuji's list.

## Capabilities

**Always**

- Live model: transform into a Pokemon in front of it, near-perfect form, basically that Pokemon's stats. [ch4] [ch5] [ch16]
- Objects and people are legal too. Early examples are clumsy (lamp, TV, cartoon-legged Amber). [ch3] By the journey they can be clean. The scarf is not a transform; it is Ditto sitting on her shoulders as a blob. [ch3] [ch5]
- Cannot copy memories, learned strategies, or complex technique — only form and biomechanics
- Standard Ditto transformations are visibly incomplete. This one's exceptional origin stays a background secret unless a scene reveals it

**Pallet attack**

- Fuji's drilled protocols were then current: Gyarados, Nidoking, Rhydon, Gengar, Arcanine, Ninetales

**Journey start**

- Those protocols are gone from disuse. Ranch aces were never kept either
- What enters the buffer depends on what Ditto wants to hold and can hold, not on automatically storing every copy
- A held form fades in about 1–3 days unless recopied
- About 10 held forms before they jumble. That cap can move if Ditto trains the memory itself; how, and whether emotion can bind forms Fuji never got, is not decided
- Toolkit: live copy, plus whatever it is currently holding. No stored protocol list
- A live strong Pokemon in front of it is still dangerous. An absent one is not
- Teammates are reliable live models. Recopying them is how a form stays in the buffer
