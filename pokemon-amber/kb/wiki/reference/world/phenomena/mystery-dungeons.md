# Mystery Dungeons

**Status:** drafting

Mystery Dungeons are ancient wilderness zones where geography, Pokemon behavior,
energy, and spatial logic become unstable. Interiors may exceed exteriors;
forests, routes, or ruins may loop and rearrange; weather and habitats can
become locally distorted. They are uncommon but known to Rangers, Centers,
researchers, trainers, and adventurers.

They distort places within the Pokemon world rather than opening into other
worlds. Ultra Wormholes, Ashura, and Laqua have separate mechanisms.

## Destabilization and Containment

Human activity does not create Dungeons but can destabilize existing sites.
Working pressures include ecological damage, energy extraction, high-energy or
wormhole experiments, legendary disruption, and wartime production; their
relative importance is open.

A destabilized Dungeon may overflow its known boundary through distorted
terrain, weather or habitat effects, displaced or aggressive Pokemon, or
looping geography. The exact effects are not yet fixed. Apparent spread may be
literal expansion, destabilization of known sites, discovery of unknown sites,
or some combination.

To "defeat" a Dungeon means containing an outbreak: rescue victims, remove
aggravating factors, and force distortions or Pokemon back from inhabited
terrain. Whether a Dungeon can be permanently destroyed is unknown.

Rangers own closures, ecosystem response, and public safety. Adventurers and the
[adventurer federation](../../organizations/adventurer-federation.md) handle
exploration, contracted fieldwork, shared reports, expeditions, and cross-border
support.

## Story Use

Mystery Dungeons support exploration, rescue, off-route danger, and encounters
with unexpected Pokemon. Alonso introduces Amber to explicit Dungeon
exploration; Amber gradually gains field and navigation experience. The first
on-page Dungeon is the genuine, undiscovered Dungeon beneath Rocket's Route 1
cave operation (Ch. 23-24): Ditto-heavy with psychic types, modest loot, and a
secondary forest exit. Its possible link to the psychic signal Rocket was
hunting remains unresolved.

Dungeon work uses existing [quest-board](../systems/trainers/quest-system.md)
categories such as Rescue, Item, Investigation, Defeat, and Escort. It creates
no universal Explorer or Adventurer license.

## Deliberate Entry and Safety

A planned entry into a known, regulated Dungeon generally requires an
[Escape Orb](../technology/escape-orbs.md). Rangers patrol those access points,
may inspect the Orb with Trainer ID and active-custody compliance, and treat an
entry without one as a civil safety violation rather than an automatic serious
crime. Accidental or forced entry is not a violation. Exact Orb mechanics and
any future hazard-rating or permit system remain open.

Team Rocket eventually possesses stronger evidence that its activity worsens
the crisis, but Rocket is neither the origin nor necessarily the only modern
contributor. The evidence and story disclosure belong in [Mystery Dungeon
Instability](../../../planning/saga-threads/mystery-dungeon-instability.md).

## Constraints

- The Route 1 Dungeon (Ch. 23-24) is a real Dungeon but a compact first instance: it teaches survival-level facts through Alonso without lore-dumping the full phenomenon, and its Mew-adjacent question stays open.
- Procedures may make Dungeons legible without solving their underlying science.
- Mystery Dungeons, Ultra Wormholes, Ashura, and Laqua remain distinct.
