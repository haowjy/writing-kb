# Quest System & Job Boards

**Status:** drafting

Pokemon Centers host paid freelance work for licensed trainers. The Celadon
board appears in [Chapter
7](https://github.com/haowjy/pokemon-amber/blob/main/story/ch7/chapter7.md);
the older request culture remains in place as newer institutions develop.

## Board Structure

| Label | Paper | Work |
|---|---|---|
| `[Item]` | White | Collection and retrieval. |
| `[Defeat]` | Orange | Drive away, relocate, capture, or calm problem Pokemon; lethal force requires explicit authorization. |
| `[Rescue]` | Yellow | Recover lost people or Pokemon, including Pokemon that [cannot be recalled while fainted](../pokemon/pokemon-fainting.md). |
| `[WANTED]` | Red | High-priority threats and fugitives. |
| `[Escort]` | Blue | Protect people or cargo. |
| `[Investigation]` | Green | Gather information, track, or inspect. |

Chapter 7 postings include a ₽15 Celadon Hills berry collection, a ₽80 Route 7
Eevee/Eeveelution defeat request, a ₽50 stolen Oddish notice, and the ₽20,000
Saffron Hypno mass-sleep bounty.

Public postings use E-through-A difficulty grades; S-class or classified work
is assigned directly. Grades warn and guide rather than automatically bar a
trainer. Posters can require team size, equipment, type expertise, or prefer
particular badges.

## License and Payment

Every Center contract requires a valid [trainer license](licensing.md). Badges
may serve as requested evidence of experience but are not a universal legal
prerequisite. Adventurer identity is separate from both the license and the
board category.

Most quests pay a fixed bounty after the trainer returns proof and the sponsor
verifies completion; hazard or urgency bonuses and item rights can vary. Quest
income can support a small team, but a full roster usually requires sponsorship,
family money, prizes, or institutional backing. Oak's support makes Amber's
full-roster journey viable.

## Sponsors and Process

Rangers, Gyms, governments, businesses, and private citizens post work. Rangers
use the boards for supplementary route, census, rescue, and wild-Pokemon tasks.
The newer [adventurer federation](../../../organizations/adventurer-federation.md)
coordinates Mystery Dungeon maps, warnings, expeditions, and cross-border
support through the existing categories rather than adding an Adventurer quest
class.

A trainer selects a posting, presents a license and any requested evidence,
receives the assignment, then returns proof for verification and payment.
Failure or repeated cancellation can damage access to later work.

Some trainers make quest work their profession rather than pursuing League
competition. Private guilds and adventurer crews may use the boards, but neither
quest-taking nor guild membership creates a new legal trainer class.
