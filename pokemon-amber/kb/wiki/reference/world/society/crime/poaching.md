# Poaching, Hunter Guilds, and Illegal Pokemon Acquisition

Defines the recent historical origin of poaching law, how illegal acquisition
works in Amber continuity, and how old hunter guilds intersect with Team Rocket
and other buyers.

## Historical Origin

Commercial Pokemon hunting was an established profession before the first
Kanto secession war. Hunters located, subdued, transported, and captured
Pokemon commissioned by trainers, clans, Gyms, researchers, breeders,
agricultural and industrial users, militaries, foreign buyers, and settlements
that needed dangerous Pokemon removed. The work was not generally classified
as poaching.

As the war escalated, Legendary Pokemon roamed unpredictably and natural
disasters repeatedly destroyed settlements, military positions, and back-line
infrastructure. Wild Pokemon unrest disrupted both armies and civilian life.
Habitat damage, human settlement, mass wartime capture, broken migration, and
Pokemon hostility were understood as overlapping parts of the crisis even
when their exact causal relationship was disputed.

Modern poaching restrictions were introduced in the postwar period, roughly
twenty years before story present, as an environmental and security settlement.
Their purpose is not only individual Pokemon welfare. They limit commercial
extraction and habitat intrusion to reduce wild unrest, protect human
settlements, stabilize Pokemon ecologies, and appease powerful Pokemon and
Pokemon communities.

The laws are new enough that older hunters remember their work being legal and
socially ordinary. Licensed relocation, nuisance-control, research, and
emergency capture remain lawful descendants of the profession. Most surviving
commercial hunter crews now skirt permits, quotas, methods, or registration,
which gives "hunter" a broadly shady but not automatically conclusive meaning.

## Core Definitions

- Hunter --- a commercial capture specialist. Historically ordinary; now a
  contested term covering lawful contractors, gray-market crews, and poachers.
- Poaching --- commercially taking, confining, transporting, registering, or
  selling Pokemon in violation of postwar capture law. Relevant factors include
  authorization, habitat/population restrictions, scale, method, repeated
  confinement, forged registration, and theft from an existing trainer.
- Black market --- informal networks that fence stolen balls, sell illegally taken Pokémon, and traffic control devices. Operate around Celadon backstreets, Vermilion docks, and route roadhouses.

## Legal Capture vs Poaching

- Ball Handshake (legal):
  - A Poké Ball must perform a registration handshake with a conscious or at‑least responsive target. The ball records species biometrics and assigns ownership to the trainer license.
  - Consent model: not literal spoken consent, but the Pokémon must not be fully collapsed; resistance and struggle are normal, but a completely fainted target cannot complete the handshake.

- Fainted Pokémon Rule:
  - Fully fainted Pokémon cannot be returned to their ball and cannot be newly captured. Their ability to dematerialize is offline until stabilized. See mechanics/pokemon-fainting.md.
  - Fainting also disables an existing active ball connection. A bonded
    partner normally relinks after recovery, but a physical captor can remove
    the Pokemon during the disabled interval and obstruct that recovery.

- Poaching Methods (illegal):
  - Blunt-force knockouts or drug sedation to absolute collapse, then physical cages. No valid handshake occurs while the target is fainted.
  - Hold the target through recovery, attempt capture when it becomes
    responsive, and restrain or faint it again after a failed attempt. Physical
    control manufactures repeated capture opportunities that an ordinary
    encounter would not provide.
  - Use the same recovery-window method to steal a registered trainer's
    Pokemon: separate it, force fainting, remove it while recall is disabled,
    block the original relink, and begin repeated new-ball attempts.
  - Forged registry entries or cloned serial numbers on balls to simulate ownership.
  - Capture in protected zones or closed seasons.

- Practical Difference:
  - Individual licensed trainers take personal custody under badge-based carry
    limits. Commercial hunters can extract for many buyers and therefore alter
    a habitat at a scale no single trainer can.
  - A normal encounter leaves a wild Pokemon opportunities to flee, defeat the
    trainer, resist a ball, or recover elsewhere after fainting. Poachers first
    eliminate escape, then continue until a ball succeeds.
  - Physical restraint is not sufficient by itself to prove poaching because
    Rangers, medical workers, researchers, and authorized relocation crews may
    confine Pokemon temporarily. Purpose, duration, reporting, method, and
    repeated recovery-cycle control distinguish lawful containment.

## Team Rocket’s Role

- Outwardly presents as a loose, old Pokemon-catching guild descended from the
  prewar commercial hunter profession.
- Squads are semi-autonomous, often not formally registered as members, and
  connected through sponsors, handlers, brokers, reputation, and authenticated
  access rather than a complete central roster.
- The loose structure grants tactical freedom and organizational plausible
  deniability. Superiors communicate priorities and prices while squads choose
  methods; successful valuable theft may be rewarded even when never ordered
  explicitly.
- Largest regional buyer and logistics hub for poached and stolen Pokemon. A
  sufficiently rich buyer can commission almost any realistically obtainable
  species or, at much greater price and risk, a particular trainer's Pokemon.
- Most squads hunt wild targets because they are easier to launder, but Rocket
  turns a blind eye to opportunistic theft of sufficiently valuable partners.
  Specialized acquisition squads deliberately steal registered Pokemon using
  the same fainting, confinement, recovery, and repeated-capture process.
- Channels: Game Corner adjacencies, shell importers at docks, and “equipment suppliers” for clone balls and jammers.
- Diversified crime: extortion, theft rings, industrial sabotage, item fencing, loan‑sharking.
- Covert research, cloning, forced evolution, Dark Balls, legendary capture,
  and Pokemon weaponization remain compartmentalized above the ordinary hunter
  network. Most poachers know Rocket tolerates theft; they do not know the full
  black-program structure.

## Illegal Control Technologies (Contraband)

- Shock harnesses and pain collars --- remote compliance via aversive conditioning; illegal for commercial sale or field use.
- Psionic dampeners --- headbands/muzzles that suppress Psychic output; illegal without medical exemption.
- Neurochemical drip rigs --- sedative micro‑pumps to keep a target sub‑responsive; treated as animal cruelty.
- Recall jammers --- block legal recalls during ambushes; often paired with Faraday crates.
- Clone balls/serial spoofers --- duplicate ball IDs or simulate registry writes; defeated by rolling‑code audits.

Rangers and police seize these items on sight; possession implies trafficking intent.

## Enforcement & Penalties

- Ranger Union focus: protected zones, route perimeters, and transport hubs. See reference/organizations/ranger-union.md.
- Penalties scale by harm: fines, license suspension, ball confiscation, and incarceration for organized trafficking or cruelty.
- Civil liability: damages to gyms, Centers, or victims; insurers subrogate aggressively.

## Rescue & Rehabilitation

- Fainted/abused Pokémon require stabilization before any legal custody decision. Centers document chain‑of‑care; Rangers seek original lawful owners when possible.
- Court outcomes: return to lawful owner, sanctuary placement, or re‑taming under certified programs. Poached stock cannot be laundered through fresh registration.

## Author Notes (Use On‑Screen)

- Show the difference: lawful capture scenes emphasize management, commands, and post‑battle care; poaching scenes show cages, sedatives, and failed recalls.
- Stakes: fainted targets can’t “escape into a ball,” making rescues urgent.
- Tech texture: drop a jammer or collar detail sparingly; avoid gadget sprawl.
