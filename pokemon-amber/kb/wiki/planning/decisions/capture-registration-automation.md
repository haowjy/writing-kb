# Capture Registration and Transfer Automation

**Status:** drafting

Settled direction for how Pokemon transfer, capture registration, and carry
enforcement evolve across the saga. Current-year rules and the next-year broad
rollout are settled; the later automation's timing, vendor, offline behavior,
and mechanism are open planning boundaries.

## Settled: Current Era (Amber's first journey year)

- A Trainer License limits Amber to **three simultaneously carried/active
  Pokemon**. Carry limits are not lifetime capture quotas.
- A trainer at capacity must open a slot --- transfer, stabling, surrender, or
  release --- **before** registering another personal capture.
- Licensed capture is generally lawful on maintained routes and open Mystery
  Dungeons unless local restrictions, protected species, closures, or seasons
  say otherwise.
- Pokemon Centers accept surrendered or transferred Pokemon for care,
  relocation, and placement.
- Pokeballs are expensive; cost is the ordinary practical brake on
  indiscriminate catching, not a legal capture quota.
- Instantaneous transfer is a limited beta at select Pokemon Centers. Trainers
  generally must know to ask. Transfers go to approved physical facilities
  such as Oak's ranch --- not indefinite digital storage.
- Amber knows about the beta through Oak and can transfer to his ranch.

## Settled: Broad Rollout (Ash's cohort year)

The Center-to-ranch instantaneous transfer network becomes broadly available
to the public the year after Amber's departure.

## Settled Direction: Registry Integration (later)

Pokedex/Pokeball/registry integration matures roughly two to three years after
Amber's departure and relaxes the pre-capture slot rule:

- Automatic capture registration
- License/capacity checks and protected-status checks where supported
- Designated receiving facilities; excess captures can auto-transfer to the
  trainer's designated facility once network coverage is available
- Inactive/deployment locks for Pokemon beyond the active carry limit;
  inactive balls cannot ordinarily be deployed, traded, or re-registered
  without authorization

**Invariants:**

- The six-Pokemon active limit remains. Technology relaxes handling of excess
  captures, not the active-team safety ceiling.
- Pokemon Centers retain emergency/welfare overrides. No Pokemon may be
  dangerously trapped in a locked ball without recourse.

**Attribution:** Oak's Pokedex work contributes identification and
field-registration architecture; do not make Oak sole inventor of the whole
system. Silph Co. or another technology provider may develop the
ball-lock/transfer protocols.

## Open Boundaries (record as open, not canon)

- Exact automation timing: two vs. three years after Amber's departure
- Vendor attribution for the ball-lock/transfer protocols
- Offline behavior and exact product implementation
- Detailed engineering mechanism

## Why

- Cost, not bureaucracy, is the era's brake on catching: it keeps early-story
  economics meaningful while letting capture legality stay permissive.
- A beta-to-public rollout explains why the familiar transfer system exists by
  the games era without anachronistically giving Amber's year-one journey a
  PC-storage network.
- Inactive locks plus Center welfare overrides let later stories use ball
  security (theft, unauthorized deployment) without ever implying a Pokemon
  can be trapped beyond help.
