## What the year is

Amber leaves Pallet at fourteen after ordinary middle-school graduation, with
Oak's blessing, a trainer license and Ditto. Oak prepared her, but this is not
an early-departure exception. She goes to see the world of Pokemon: the same
childhood dream she had before the timeskip, and the same curiosity that got
her into trouble.

She is equipped but untested. Game knowledge is not wilderness experience, and
the early journey shows the gap between knowing type matchups and knowing how
to camp, navigate and read wild Pokemon. It is not desperate survival: Oak's
support means she is properly resourced, working-class-plus rather than
wealthy.

Her plan is the normal circuit: eight badges and Conference qualification.
Incidents keep pulling her off it, and the gap between the plan and what keeps
happening is where episodes come from. Amber is not a chosen one. She gets
pulled in because she is good with Pokemon, Pokemon with problems trust her,
and she cannot walk away from one in trouble.

## Pallet cohort

Oak sponsors three graduates of Amber's class: Amber, Reid and Nora, all
fourteen. Ash and Gary are thirteen, a school year behind. The three Pokeballs
on Oak's pedestal (Cyndaquil, Eevee, Electrike) are for this year's graduates;
Amber declines one and keeps Ditto [ch18 draft]. Which graduate takes which starter
is open.

Amber is a loner at school, so Reid and Nora are off the page in chapter 18.
For now they wait for a later episode that uses them; the author is trying an
introduction where Amber doesn't remember who they are. Their current roles:
Reid is a fiery, competitive conventional rival; Nora is an analytical,
precise foil, not a promised battle rival. Most familiar tournament opponents
should be people Amber meets on the road rather than Pallet classmates.

## Travelling group

Amber and Kyle meet on Route 1 in the Mew incident and continue together
provisionally. Alonso joins them on Route 2 through the Teddiursa rescue, and
the three travel together through most of Kanto by repeated choice rather than
a pact. Alonso is the free adventurer who draws them toward Mystery Dungeons;
he has his own goal, mistakes and progression, and could be the protagonist of
his own story. Near Kanto's end, Charcadet chooses Amber, Alonso lets it go, and
he leaves for his next adventure; Amber and Kyle continue as a duo. Prince
Alexander travels a separate route and meets them as a mysterious rival.

Local companions should usually keep one local function and maybe one focal
Pokemon rather than growing a permanent cast.

## Amber's Pokemon this year

- **Ditto**: her partner from Arc 0, registered on her license [ch18 draft].
- **Caterpie**: first catch on Route 1 (ch19). See
  [Caterpie's courage](../../team-arcs/amber/caterpie-courage.md).
- **Teddiursa**: joins through her promise to help rescue his mother from
  Rocket on Route 2. Grievance-driven, not a comfort bear; he tolerates Amber
  because she moves toward the problem. The promise stays unfulfilled through
  Kanto. See [Ursaring rescue](ursaring-rescue.md).
- **Heracross**: captured in a prepared Dungeon after Route 2 (ch29).
- **Charcadet**: Alonso's younger Charcadet. Its interest starts when it sees
  Amber protect a fleeing Teddiursa; its choice develops across Kanto and lands
  near the end, shortly before 4Ever. The bond comes from what it watches her
  do, never from an object. See
  [Charcadet / Ceruledge](../../saga-threads/charcadet-ceruledge.md).

The license allows three active Pokemon; Giovanni's badge raises Amber's
allowance to four, which carries Ditto, Caterpie, Teddiursa and Heracross.

## Mew on Route 1

Mew is curious, playful and free to leave whenever it likes. It takes an
intense interest in Amber that neither she nor Kyle can explain; the hidden
reason may involve her trace Mew-derived biology. The scene reveals no clone
lore. The Rocket grunts see enough to report Mew's interest in a girl with a
Ditto, which seeds later surveillance. They are a small field squad, separate
from the larger Route 2 operation.

## Viridian badge

Viridian is Amber's first badge, not her eighth. Giovanni is never seen in the
cave or with Team Rocket. He meets Amber, Kyle and Alonso at the secondary
Route 2 exit and attacks at once with Nidoking without introducing himself.
After Amber and Alonso do most of the work to beat it, he announces himself as
Viridian's Gym Leader and calls it an impromptu test. He gives standard badges
to Amber and Alonso, pauses before Kyle, gives him nothing, and leaves: no tour,
no invitation back.

Amber knows who Giovanni is and that his attention is dangerous, but cannot
prove his link to the cave. Her reaction runs relief, real excitement over her
first badge, then dry apprehension that her careful journey already looks like
Ash's. Whether Giovanni recognizes her as Fuji's creation is open.

## Badges and the Conference

Amber pursues eight badges. She wins when she can actually challenge; the
bottleneck is time and disruption, not weakness. Gym waits run two to four
weeks for pillar Gyms, cooldowns a week to a month, and travel days to weeks on
foot. Any eight Gyms qualify; Viridian is first.

## Kyle and Saffron

Kyle Kong is the Fighting Dojo heir, fourteen, a standard-age starter with
family resources: reckless, physically strong, tactically weak. Saffron's
Gym politics make the Conference a proxy war between the Fighting Dojo and
Sabrina's Psychic Gym. Amber's tactics help him reach eight badges; Alonso's
freely chosen life shows him he can want something beyond obligation. Kyle
defeats Sabrina at the critical public moment, which delays the Dojo's
displacement rather than preventing it, then rejects inheriting the Dojo and
wants to keep travelling with Amber. Without Amber he would lose to Sabrina and
the Dojo would lose its position at once. See
[Saffron Gym rivalry](saffron-gym-rivalry.md).

Kyle gets lost constantly, for comedy and as a way to split and rejoin the
group.

## Team Rocket

An escalating background threat, not the year's engine. The Route 1 and
Route 2 reports (Mew's interest, a compromised cave operation, escaped
witnesses, a foreign adventurer, two Charcadet, Ursaring extracted) sit in
Rocket files. The grunts embarrassed by a fourteen-year-old hold a low-level
grudge. Organizational pursuit comes much later, when old records trace back
to the youngest Hoenn Conference winner. Use Butch and Cassidy or other agents,
not a Jessie-and-James comedy duo.
