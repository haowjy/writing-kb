# Caterpie / Butterfree: Cowardice and Courage

## Arc in One Line

Caterpie turns silk developed for escaping danger into a way to move toward danger, then must make Amber recognize that its ambitions have changed.

## Starting State

- Caterpie is physically weak, openly afraid of direct confrontation, and ashamed that former peers evolved while it remained behind.
- It trusts *String Shot* because precise silk, stealth, and retreat kept it fed and alive.
- It joins Amber for food and safety rather than a desire to battle.
- Amber initially reads Caterpie accurately as reluctant capture support.

See [Caterpie's character reference](../../../reference/characters/pokemon/amber-team/caterpie.md) for stable personality and survival history.

## Chapter 20 Seed

Chapter 20 shows Caterpie's silk-based survival, shame at being left behind, refusal to spar, and first attempt to swing between branches. Caterpie misses its second attachment and crashes. Amber runs over, confirms it is unhurt, and laughs; Caterpie chuckles with her. The failure becomes shared play instead of another humiliation.

Their noise attracts a predatory Pidgey. Ditto intercepts as Pidgey, then changes into Caterpie to restrain the fleeing attacker with *String Shot*. Caterpie sees its own form used effectively without being forced to fight. Pidgey breaks out of Amber's capture attempt, sheds the external restraint through dematerialization, and escapes.

The chapter opens the possibility of training without resolving the fear. Scene execution belongs in the [Chapter 20 plan](https://github.com/haowjy/pokemon-amber/blob/main/story/ch20/plan.md).

## Progression

1. **Avoidance:** Caterpie contributes from safety and uses silk to escape.
2. **Experiment:** Silk becomes movement rather than only restraint.
3. **Practice:** Caterpie improves placement, release timing, and consecutive swings while remaining frightened of direct battle.
4. **Support:** *String Shot* helps slow, restrain, and reposition capture targets.
5. **Protective choice:** Caterpie eventually swings toward danger to protect Amber or the party.
6. **Metapod evolution:** Caterpie evolves during the committed swing. Existing silk preserves momentum; Metapod's increased mass and *Harden* produce an improvised pendulum strike.
7. **Butterfree utility:** Compound Eyes, powders, flight, scouting, and silk make Butterfree excellent support.
8. **Stale assessment:** As stronger battlers join, Amber repeatedly rotates or benches Butterfree while continuing to treat its original refusal as permanent.
9. **Relationship conflict:** Butterfree feels guilty remaining safe and frustrated that Amber never asks again whether it wants direct opportunities.
10. **Reassessment:** Butterfree must express its changed desire; Amber must stop treating an accurate first impression as a fixed identity.

## Emotional Logic

Caterpie does contribute. Its insecurity comes from believing that support from safety does not count beside teammates who take hits and win visible battles.

Its frustration points both inward and toward Amber:

- guilt when teammates return injured;
- envy of direct training and victories;
- anger at itself for earlier avoidance;
- anger at Amber for continuing to see the Pokemon it used to be.

Courage means acting while afraid. The arc should never transform Caterpie into a fearless conventional brawler.

## Former-Peer Mirror

A later Viridian Forest encounter may place one of Caterpie's former peers, now a Butterfree caught by a Bug Catcher, opposite it. The peer should function as a mirror rather than a simple villain. Whether it was actively cruel or merely larger and more competitive remains open.

## Planned Payoffs

- Metapod pendulum evolution while protecting the party.
- A later Compound Eyes plus powder tournament performance that gives Butterfree a visible victory rather than using it only as a matchup tool.
- Eventual rotation or residence at Oak's ranch after the relationship conflict is resolved.

## Placement Constraints

- Do not complete the courage arc in Chapter 20.
- Do not place the Metapod evolution inside the already crowded Team Rocket camp or Ursaring cave sequence without reconsidering Teddiursa, Alonso, and Charcadet's competing beats.
- Seed changes across repeated behavior rather than introducing guilt immediately before its payoff.

## Open Questions

- Exact former-peer encounter in Viridian Forest.
- When repeated benching begins.
- Whether the tournament performance resolves the Amber conflict or follows an earlier reconciliation.
