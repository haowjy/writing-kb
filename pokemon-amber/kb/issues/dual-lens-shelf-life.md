# Gamer Voice Rule

**Resolved from:** Dual-Lens Shelf Life Question (2026-07)  
**Status:** Settled rule. The open question is closed; this is now durable KB memory.  
**Scope:** Amber's first-person narration and discovery scenes.  

---

## The Rule

Amber's gamer voice decays as she acclimates to the Pokemon world. Early chapters carry it heavily; it thins naturally across the story. By the post-time-skip era (Ch. 18 onward), Pokemon is her lived reality and the gamer lens is gone.

### Keep

- **Plot-functional foreknowledge** — game knowledge that drives action, decisions, or stakes (type matchups, move data, evolution requirements).
- **Meaningful game/reality corrections** — where game knowledge is wrong or incomplete and the correction reveals something about the world.
- **Comparisons that expose Amber's imperfect mental model** — when the gap between what she expects and what she finds is the point.

### Cut

- **Decorative gamer metaphors** — gaming terminology used as color rather than substance ("critical hit" as description, "NPC" for a passerby).
- **Let's Play-style narration** — play-by-play commentary that reads like a streamer reacting to a game.
- **Redundant echoes** — repeating the same game comparison or structural beat an earlier chapter already covered.
- **Duplicate narrative events** — e.g., duplicate Ditto emergence (Ch. 8, removed) and duplicate Tangela return (Ch. 10, removed).

### Decay Curve

| Phase | Chapters | Gamer Voice Density |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy | 1–5 | Game references are frequent; the dual-lens is the dominant discovery mode |
| Moderate | 7–10 | Thins naturally; first-contact comparisons are earned, not decorative |
| Sparse | 11–17 | References appear when they serve character or plot, not worldbuilding |
| Gone | 18+ (post-time-skip) | No decorative gamer metaphors. Foreknowledge may still matter as plot substance |

### Non-Amber POV

Chapters from other characters' POV (Fuji, Oak, third-person limited) carry **no gamer voice**. No game comparisons, no meta-references, no dual-lens framing. Amber's game knowledge is exclusively hers.

### Intentional Repetition

Intentional repetition can remain when it creates comic rhythm or character pressure. This is a voice judgment, not a blanket ban on repeated sentence shapes.

## Chapter Titles

Unchanged. `Truck-kun Strikes Again` and the Side Quest titles may retain their ironic/light genre register. The gamer-voice rule governs prose narration, not title tone.
