# Mechanical Tics — Repeated Phrases

**Scope:** Word- and phrase-level repetition across revision passes
**Severity:** Low to moderate when repetition becomes noticeable

## Problem

A familiar phrase, hedge, perception anchor, or delayed-reaction construction
can become automatic. The issue is not the word by itself; it is a recurrence
that gives no new timing, uncertainty, orientation, or voice.

Examples worth search-and-evaluate include "for a moment," the "cut through"
family, "before I could," repeated eyes/voice anchors, and hedges such as
"probably," "kind of," and "maybe." Counts age quickly as the manuscript
changes, so this issue does not retain unversioned totals.

## Revision question

For each hit, ask what it does here:

- Does it establish duration, interruption, uncertainty, perception, or a
  character's habitual voice?
- Does it orient a conversation or action in a way another detail would not?
- Is it merely a generic bridge after the surrounding prose already does that
  work?

Delete an unnecessary beat before substituting a synonym. Keep meaningful
hedges and perception anchors; they are not prohibited vocabulary. When a
pattern is real, vary the underlying attention or action rather than conducting
a mechanical thesaurus pass.
