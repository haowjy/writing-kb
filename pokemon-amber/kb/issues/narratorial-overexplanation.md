# Narratorial Overexplanation

**Scope:** Recurring in Chs. 10, 11, 14, and 17

**Severity:** Moderate — it can place interpretation between the event and the reader

## Problem

A scene sometimes renders its mood or relationship, then adds stacked imagery,
a diagnosis, a performed joke, or an emotional caption that explains the same
thing again. The first rendering can be vivid; the extra layer narrows the
reader's room to infer.

## Evidence

- Ch. 10 stacks atmosphere onto its approach: "The neon grew sicker," "angry
  electricity," and a lock that sounds like "grinding teeth." [ch10]
- Ch. 11 labels the confrontation with "words fell like broken glass" and
  "this broken family tableau" after the dialogue and staging already carry
  the conflict. [ch11]
- Ch. 14 explicitly contrasts Amber's adult mind with bodily delight, then
  returns to the same framing around the syrup scene. [ch14]
- Ch. 17 captions visible shifts as warmth vanishing, a guarded look, and
  "the vast emptiness in her eyes." [ch17]

## Revision direction

Ask what the sentence contributes to the passage: a distinct perception,
consequence, choice, turn, voice, rhythm, or a necessary connection. A sentence
can earn its place by connecting existing information without adding a new
fact. Remove or compress it when it contributes neither, rather than swapping
in a new metaphor or gesture. Plain telling is allowed.

## Counter-risk: disconnected prose

Do not treat the request to remove AI slop as a request for maximum compression.
A memory's trigger, a return to the present, or the thought leading to a choice
can be necessary even when the reader already knows the underlying facts.
Cutting these can leave individually competent sentences that do not follow
from one another.

Judge the revised paragraph and its neighbors, not only the changed line.
Reorder related details, restore a missing link, or separate different subjects
as needed. Adding "then" or an arbitrary gesture does not establish a missing
relationship. See [paragraph movement](../styles/tone.md#paragraph-movement).

## Emotional latitude

This is not a demand that Amber break down, declare shock, stop thinking about
Pokemon, or display a prescribed psychology under grief. Observational distance,
uncertainty, game thought, direct feeling words, and bodily response can all be
right when the scene earns them. The open question is calibration of the
specific moment, not an absent universal emotional register.
