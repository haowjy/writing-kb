# Amber: Noncanonical First-Person Voice Samples

Use this file when calibrating Amber's current narration voice or drafting new Amber POV prose. These samples are synthetic and noncanonical. They demonstrate choices, not reusable description. Each annotation explains why the paragraph, line, action, and significant phrase exists and what it contributes to the narrative, tone, or story beat. If the scene does not supply that purpose, omit the detail.

---

## Quiet observation

> The Pokemon Center waiting room had the same smell as every other one: disinfectant, wet fur, and somebody trying too hard to look calm. A girl in a school sweater kept bouncing one knee under the chair. Her Pidgey pressed one wing against the glass and watched the door like it expected bad news. Nobody here had come for nothing. That was the whole problem.

**Use when:** Amber is waiting for news about an injured Pokemon. Her own worry makes her notice the restrained worry of the trainers and Pokemon around her.

**Choice by choice:**
- **"The Pokemon Center waiting room"** establishes the shared situation immediately. The scene needs a place where several people could be awaiting medical news.
- **"had the same smell as every other one"** shows familiarity. Amber has lived in this world long enough for Pokemon Centers to feel ordinary.
- **"disinfectant, wet fur"** grounds the room in medical cleanliness and the physical presence of Pokemon.
- **"somebody trying too hard to look calm"** turns the sensory list toward Amber's actual concern: everyone in the room is worried.
- **The girl's bouncing knee** gives that worry a visible action instead of naming her anxiety.
- **The school sweater** makes her an ordinary person interrupted in the middle of ordinary life. Keep it only if that contrast matters to Amber in the scene.
- **The Pidgey's wing against the glass** gives it physical contact with whatever separates it from treatment or news.
- **Watching the door** mirrors the girl's waiting and directs the reader toward the expected arrival of medical news.
- **"Nobody here had come for nothing"** broadens Amber's observation from one pair to the room.
- **"That was the whole problem"** returns the observation to Amber's own stakes. This closing line belongs only when she is also waiting for someone injured; without that pressure, it overstates the room.

## Social inference

> The breeder's smile arrived a second late, as if he had to remember to wear it. He said the egg was "fine" and touched the blanket without looking down. I kept my face open and asked about the heat lamp first. People told the truth faster when they thought you were being practical.

**Use when:** Amber suspects someone is concealing a problem with a Pokemon egg and needs information without provoking them.

**Choice by choice:**
- **The delayed smile** is the first observable mismatch between the breeder's words and behavior.
- **"as if he had to remember to wear it"** gives Amber's interpretation without treating it as proven fact.
- **Calling the egg "fine"** supplies the claim Amber is testing.
- **Touching the blanket without looking down** gives her a second tell and keeps the hidden egg physically present in the exchange.
- **Keeping her face open** is an action Amber controls. It prevents her suspicion from closing the conversation.
- **Asking about the heat lamp** makes her strategy concrete: begin with a practical detail adjacent to the suspected problem.
- **The final sentence** explains the principle guiding her action after the prose has demonstrated it. Use it only if Amber's deliberate social strategy matters to the beat.

## Pressure / danger

> The hallway shook once. Dust came loose from the doorframe and drifted down in a thin gray line. The Growlithe on the tile went silent, which was worse than barking. I crouched by instinct and held one hand out to stop the others from crowding the corner. Was that a threat or a warning? I did not plan to find out while standing up.

**Use when:** Amber is protecting other people in an unstable space and does not yet know what caused the disturbance.

**Choice by choice:**
- **The single shake** starts with an event rather than an explanation.
- **Dust falling from the frame** confirms that the movement affected the building and gives Amber something visible to track.
- **The Growlithe going silent** matters because a watchful Pokemon's behavior can warn Amber before she understands the threat.
- **"which was worse than barking"** records her immediate judgment of that change.
- **Crouching** lowers her profile before she has time to reason through the danger.
- **Holding out one hand** turns instinct into protection by stopping the others from bunching at the corner.
- **The question** preserves uncertainty about what lies ahead.
- **Refusing to investigate while exposed** converts that uncertainty into a practical choice.

## Functional game knowledge

> The Geodude pulled its arms tight against its chest. Defense Curl. If it started rolling in this hallway, the ankles would go first. I told everyone to back against the walls and clear the floor. It could have the hallway. I wanted our bones intact.

**Use when:** Amber recognizes a move early enough to prevent nearby people from being struck.

**Choice by choice:**
- **The Geodude's arms tightening** gives Amber the physical cue before she identifies the move.
- **"Defense Curl"** is the relevant piece of game knowledge. Its isolation makes recognition immediate without opening a statistical explanation.
- **The hallway and exposed ankles** translate the move into the actual geometry and danger of the scene.
- **Backing against the walls and clearing the floor** shows the knowledge changing Amber's instructions.
- **Letting it have the hallway** prioritizes safety over winning ground.
- **Wanting intact bones** states the serious consequence plainly. The blunt practicality carries the deadpan edge without turning the danger into a joke.

## Deadpan humor

> The Magnemite had attached itself to the refrigerator and stayed there through two people, three complaints, and one very serious threat involving a broom. The landlord kept asking if it had always done that. I checked the dented door, the dead magnet strip, and the little thing humming with complete confidence. "Looks permanent," I said. The landlord stopped asking about the Magnemite and started reading the lease.

**Use when:** A mundane adult problem has become absurd because a Pokemon is involved, while Amber remains responsible for dealing with the consequences.

**Choice by choice:**
- **The Magnemite on the refrigerator** supplies the absurd situation without describing it as absurd.
- **Two people, three complaints, and the broom threat** escalates failed responses in a factual count. Calling the broom threat "very serious" keeps the delivery straight.
- **The landlord's repeated question** shows denial and gives Amber a practical problem beyond removing the Pokemon.
- **The dented door and dead magnet strip** establish real property damage.
- **The Magnemite's confident humming** contrasts with the damaged appliance without asking the reader to laugh.
- **"Looks permanent"** answers the landlord literally and leaves the larger implication unstated.
- **Reading the lease** delivers the consequence as an action. The prose never explains the joke or announces a punchline.
