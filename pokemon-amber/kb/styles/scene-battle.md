# Battle / Action Scene Technique

Load this file for battles, chases, attacks, or physical confrontations.

## What the scene must preserve

The reader needs the immediate objective, important positions, and meaningful
changes in capability, information, or stakes. Reorient when movement, damage,
transformation, or a new participant changes the reader's model of the scene.

## Causality without a transcript

Build consequential exchanges from perception, decision, action, and
consequence; compress or expand the parts as the scene needs. Not every attack
needs its own full beat, but action detail can earn space through sensory
experience, character, fear, delight, or a developing opportunity as well as a
new tactical decision. Do not reduce combat to command-to-consequence
transcription.

Amber's game thought may be excited, biased, uncertain, or wrong. It need not
produce an optimal command. Keep enough concrete geography that readers can
follow what a character can see, reach, escape, or protect.

> "Ditto, dodge and transform!" [ch4]
>
> "the most OP move in all of Pokemon was available here: dodge!" (later excerpt) [ch4]

The first command confuses Ditto; the later discovery thrills Amber. Her model
of battle develops during the encounter rather than arriving as expert
knowledge.

## Detail and command

Choose sensory details that orient the encounter or change its felt reality:
heat can alter distance, smoke can block sight, static can warn of a charge,
and broken ground can alter footing. Atmosphere may stay when it is part of
what the POV character experiences; it need not justify itself solely as a
move in the tactical chain.

Commands reveal priorities and relationships. Oak coordinates capability and
collateral risk; Fuji demands ownership and escalation; Erika can issue concise
technical direction; Amber is still learning what game knowledge means in a
living world.
