# Chapter 1 Replacement Titles

## Intent

The requested change is a tonal recalibration: retain room for Amber's game-literate voice, but make the first promise the tank, uncertain identity, and uncanny Mewtwo encounter rather than a familiar isekai-impact joke.

## Grounding

- The revised opening begins with bodily disorientation in a tank; the horn and headlights are only a clipped remembered fragment.
- The title slate for Chapters 2-17 ranges from plain place/character titles to lightly game-inflected irony, so a modestly meta option can fit without making Chapter 1 a genre-parody outlier.
- <hidden>The digital Shadow Mewtwo / real Mewtwo juxtaposition and the name Amber are early signals; the old-world origin is not to be fully announced, and later confirmation must remain unspoiled.</hidden>

## Options

### 1. Gamer-language / double meaning

<AI>
- **New Game+** — Foregrounds a life that feels restarted while preserving the protagonist's gamer register; risk: its familiar gaming term may still imply a conventional isekai reset.
- **No Menu Appeared** — Foregrounds the collision between game expectations and physical danger, especially at the first sight of Mewtwo; risk: it is a slightly longer, deliberately jokey line.
- **Save State** — Foregrounds the uncertainty of a consciousness, body, or identity carried forward into a new situation; risk: it may suggest more continuity/control over the old life than the chapter confirms.
</AI>

### 2. Mewtwo or clone resonance

<AI>
- **Shadow to Flesh** — Foregrounds the digital Shadow Mewtwo memory becoming a terrifying physical Mewtwo; risk: its poetic contrast may overstate a literal connection between the two.
- **Residual Memory** — Foregrounds the flicker of recognition and the chapter's unstable identities without explaining them; risk: it sounds clinical and can make readers hunt for a mystery before the scene earns it.
- **Second Creation** — Foregrounds Amber and Mewtwo as unnatural lives emerging from the same disaster; risk: it leans toward revealing the cloning premise rather than letting the tank scene uncover it.
</AI>

### 3. Sensory / mystery / dread

<AI>
- **Orange-Tinted Shadows** — Foregrounds the opening's blurred tank vision and its ominous uncertainty; risk: it is atmospheric enough that it gives little genre or character signal.
- **The Sound in the Glass** — Foregrounds the tank's mounting tremors and the impossible voice before the break; risk: it may make the chapter sound more horror-forward than its broader adventure tone.
- **Violet Eyes** — Foregrounds the immediate, frightening recognition between Amber and Mewtwo; risk: it identifies the central visual more directly than a mystery title and reads somewhat melodramatic.
- **Before the Glass Breaks** — Foregrounds suspended dread and the tank awakening's irreversible threshold; risk: it is more literary and less aligned with the plainer, game-aware neighboring titles.
</AI>

## Open Questions

- Does the title need to signal a game-aware narrator before the prose makes that voice clear, or should the chapter earn that register after its horror-tinged opening?
- Should the Mewtwo echo be legible on the title page, or work only as a discovery inside the chapter?
- Is Chapter 1 meant to stand apart as a sharper mystery/horror hook, or establish the lightly playful naming pattern seen in Chapters 2-10 immediately?
